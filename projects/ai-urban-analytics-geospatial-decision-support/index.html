  1. ACDSA
    Urban Representation Learning for Identifying Potential Disparities in Crime Reporting
    Jorge Valverde-Rebaza, C. Paez De La Cruz, P. Pérez-Collado, D. Sánchez-Cataño, and E. Martı́nez-Vergara
    In Proceedings of the International Conference on Artificial Intelligence, Computer, Data Sciences and Applications (ACDSA 2027), 2027
    Accepted, to be published
    Abs Bib PDF Code

    Open crime data support urban safety analysis but reflect reported incidents rather than the complete distribution of criminal activity, potentially introducing spatial distortions. We propose a self-supervised urban representation learning framework that identifies unusual reporting patterns relative to representation-similar peers. Multi-scale reporting profiles are encoded into a latent urban representation, while Neighbor Profile Divergence (NPD) quantifies deviations from peer-expected behavior. Experiments on more than 1.8 million crime records from Mexico City show that the proposed representation achieves a mean Silhouette score of 0.4052, compared with 0.2863 for Hex2Vec. NPD rankings remain stable across neighborhood sizes, with Spearman correlations from 0.9720 to 0.9951. High-divergence regions exhibit spatially organized patterns and differences in reporting structure and incident volume, supporting their use as candidates for further investigation.
