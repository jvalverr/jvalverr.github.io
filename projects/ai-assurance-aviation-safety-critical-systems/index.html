AI Assurance for Aviation and Safety-Critical Systems

Research on trustworthy and reliable AI for aviation and safety-critical systems, with a focus on decision assurance, human oversight and the operational validation of Generative AI outputs.

Investigating how Generative AI can support high-stakes decisions while preserving reliability, human oversight and compliance with operational constraints.

Overview

Generative AI and Large Language Models are increasingly being considered for decision-support tasks in domains where errors can have significant operational or safety consequences.

Aviation provides a particularly demanding environment for this research. Activities such as flight planning require AI-generated recommendations to remain consistent, interpretable and compliant with strict operational and regulatory constraints. A fluent or apparently plausible response is not necessarily a safe or operationally valid one.

This project investigates AI assurance mechanisms for aviation and other safety-critical systems, focusing on how probabilistic AI outputs can be evaluated before they influence operational decisions.

The broader objective is to develop methods that bridge the gap between the flexibility of Generative AI and the deterministic reliability requirements of high-stakes environments.

Research questions

The project explores questions such as:

  • How can the reliability of AI-generated recommendations be assessed before operational use?
  • How can semantic stability and operational consistency be measured across equivalent AI interactions?
  • How can domain-specific safety and regulatory constraints be incorporated into AI evaluation?
  • How should uncertainty influence the level of authority granted to an AI-generated recommendation?
  • How can human oversight be calibrated according to the reliability of AI outputs?
  • How can assurance mechanisms remain model-independent and transferable across safety-critical domains?

Current research direction

The project currently focuses on decision assurance, operational consistency and constraint-aware evaluation of Generative AI in aviation.

The research is expanding toward adaptive assurance mechanisms, adversarial robustness, longitudinal reliability and validation with real operational data across safety-critical environments.

    Decision Assurance for AI-Assisted Flight Plan Generation in Air Traffic Management
    A. Barreto, S. Matsumoto, Jorge Valverde-Rebaza, C. Ataide, and P. Costa
    In Proceedings of the International Conference on Artificial Intelligence, Computer, Data Sciences and Applications (ACDSA 2027), 2027
    Accepted, to be published
    Abs Bib PDF

    Generative AI is increasingly being used informally in Air Traffic Management (ATM) for tasks such as flight plan generation, trajectory interpretation, and constraint checking. Although these tools can reduce workload and accelerate planning, their non-deterministic outputs create safety and operational risks in human-in-the-loop settings. This paper proposes the AI Trust and Assurance Layer (ATAL), a model-agnostic decision assurance architecture that evaluates whether AI-generated flight-planning outputs are sufficiently reliable for operational use. ATAL combines semantic stability under prompt variation, operational consistency of structured outputs, and normative constraint validation against domain rules, and maps these signals to a Decision Readiness Level (DRL) for human operators. An ATM-inspired experimental study shows how unsafe, inconsistent, or misleading outputs can be identified before influencing flight-plan validation or execution. Although demonstrated in aviation, the framework is also transferable to other safety-critical decision-support domains that require human oversight under regulatory constraints.
