AI for Cyberlearning and Data Analytics Education

Research on Cyberlearning, Learning Analytics and AI-assisted education, focusing on student success, educational innovation, data-driven learning environments and the responsible integration of Artificial Intelligence into higher education.

Using Artificial Intelligence and Learning Analytics to understand, support and improve learning experiences, student success and educational decision-making.

ONGOING AI FOR EDUCATION LEARNING ANALYTICS AI-ASSISTED EDUCATION STUDENT SUCCESS


Project details

  • Role: Research Collaborator
  • Institution: Tecnológico de Monterrey
  • Period: 2024–Present

Overview

This research line investigates how Artificial Intelligence, Learning Analytics and data-driven methods can support teaching, learning and educational decision-making in higher education.

The work combines educational research with Data Science and Artificial Intelligence to study student success, academic risk, learning models, educational technology and the use of AI-based tools in learning environments.

A central objective is to move beyond the use of AI merely as an automation tool and investigate how data and intelligent technologies can help educators and institutions better understand how students learn, which factors influence academic outcomes and how educational interventions can be designed more effectively.

The project also explores how Generative AI and interactive technologies can be integrated into educational experiences while preserving meaningful learning, human judgment and pedagogical objectives.

Research questions

The project explores questions such as:

  • How can Learning Analytics help identify patterns associated with student success and academic risk?
  • Which demographic, academic and co-curricular factors influence educational outcomes?
  • How can Artificial Intelligence support teaching and learning without replacing the cognitive processes required for meaningful learning?
  • How do students interact with AI-assisted educational environments compared with traditional learning models?
  • How can educational data support more inclusive and evidence-based interventions?
  • How can Generative AI and interactive technologies improve learning in STEM and other higher-education contexts?
  • How can predictive analytics support early identification of students at risk of academic failure?

Current research direction

The project currently focuses on Learning Analytics, student success, AI-assisted education and the evaluation of emerging learning technologies in higher education.

The research is expanding toward predictive educational analytics, adaptive learning environments, Generative AI in teaching and evidence-based models for supporting student learning and academic success.

Research outputs

  • Predictive Learning Analytics models for identifying patterns associated with academic success and risk of failure across different academic disciplines.
  • Data-driven studies of demographic, socioeconomic and co-curricular factors influencing student outcomes.
  • Evaluation of Generative AI and AI chatbots as educational support tools.
  • Analysis of challenge-based learning models and their impact on student performance and competency development.
  • Investigation of visuo-haptic and AI-assisted learning experiences in introductory STEM education.
  • Learning Analytics studies supporting a broader understanding of academic success beyond traditional indicators such as GPA.

Research artifacts

  • Predictive Learning Analytics models for student success and academic risk.
  • Educational datasets covering academic, demographic and co-curricular variables.
  • Analytical frameworks for evaluating student learning and educational interventions.
  • AI-assisted learning activities combining Generative AI and interactive educational technologies.

  1. EDUCON
    Student Academic Success and Risk of Failure: A Predictive Learning Analytics Study Across Engineering, Business, and Social Sciences Programs
    Andres Gonzalez-Nucamendi, Vı́ctor Robledo-Rella, Jorge Valverde-Rebaza, Julieta Noguez, Luis Neri, and Rosa M.G. Garcı́a-Castelán
    In 2026 IEEE Global Engineering Education Conference (EDUCON), 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This study investigates student academic success and the risk of failure through a predictive learning analytics approach applied to 8,428 undergraduate students from the Engineering and Science School (EIC), the Business School (EN), and the Social Sciences and Government School (ECSG) from Tecnologico de Monterrey, gathered from 2019 to 2022. Using aggregated institutional records, students were classified into three mutually exclusive academic profiles (Successful, Normal, and At-Risk) based on objective institutional academic criteria. Descriptive statistics and correlation matrices were used to examine differences in academic performance and instructional structures across schools and student profiles. At the same time, a predictive regression model was employed to identify key factors associated with students’ final course grades. Results indicate that Successful students exhibit highly consistent academic trajectories, whereas At-Risk students display more heterogeneous patterns and weaker associations between academic performance and instructional activity indicators. Substantial differences in instructional activity load and task configurations were observed across schools. The EN School presented the richest and most consistent activity structure, followed by the EIC and ECSG Schools. The predictive model identified prior GPA as the strongest predictor of final course grades, complemented by smaller but meaningful contributions from school membership, instructional activity load, and full-time student enrollment load. These findings highlight the combined influence of disciplinary context and course-level instructional structure on academic trajectories, demonstrating the value of predictive learning analytics for informing targeted academic support and evidence-based program improvement.

  2. EDUCON
    An Experience of Visuo-Haptic Activities with AI in Engineering and Architecture Introductory Physics Courses
    Rosa Marı́a G. Garcı́a-Castelán, Andres Gonzalez-Nucamendi, Luis Neri, Vı́ctor Robledo-Rella, Julieta Noguez, and Jorge Valverde-Rebaza
    In 2026 IEEE Global Engineering Education Conference (EDUCON), 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This work in progress aims to evaluate the impact of combining Visuo-Haptic Simulations (VHSs) and ChatGPT on engineering and architecture students’ learning of force analysis in introductory physics courses. This effort investigates: (1) how VHS and ChatGPT affect the understanding of force analysis, (2) whether gender differences exist in the learning process, and (3) whether outcomes differ across disciplines. Using a pre-test/post-test design, experimental groups (95 students) and control groups (105 students) were studied along Fall-2023, Spring-2024 and Fall-2024 terms at a private Mexican university. Experimental groups answered a pre-test, used a VHS of a block on an inclined plane, completed a related questionnaire, answered a post-test1, compared their work with ChatGPT outcomes, answered a post-test2, and explained discrepancies. Control groups had a traditional instruction. For the experimental group average scores in a 0-5 scale rose from 2.22 (pre-test) up to 3.27 (post-test 1) and 3.92 (post-test 2), with p<0.001 in all comparisons, emphasizing the effectiveness of VHS and ChatGPT activities. No significant differences were found between genders or between engineering and architecture students.

  1. ICERI
    Gender-Based Comparison of Evaluations Using Visuo-Haptic Simulators and ChatGPT Activities Versus Traditional Learning Models
    R.M.G. Garcia-Castelan, A. González-Nucamendi, L.J. Neri-Vitela, V.F. Robledo-Rella, J.J. Noguez-Monroy, and J.C. Valverde-Rebaza
    In ICERI2025 Proceedings, Nov 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Physical interaction enhances cognitive processes, and visuo-haptic simulators (VHS)—which integrate the sense of touch with visual simulations—offer a more engaging approach to learning abstract concepts. In particular, understanding the forces acting on a block on an inclined plane becomes more intuitive in a VHS environment compared to solely solving textbook problems. This study involved 19 first-year engineering students enrolled in an introductory Physics course. After receiving a lecture on the topic, the students engaged in a VHS activity and used ChatGPT to further interpret and discuss their findings. Their performance was compared to a control group of 28 first-year engineering students who followed a traditional instruction model and completed the same written evaluation. The groups consisted of 32% female students in the experimental group, while only 21% in the control group, highlighting the still persistent gender gap in engineering education. This paper presents an analysis of pre- and post-test results from the experimental group, as well as a comparative assessment of male and female students’ performance in both instructional models. Preliminary results revealed no statistically significant differences in learning gains between male and female students within the experimental group (p = 0.487).

  2. EDUCON
    Can We Trust AI Chatbots to Teach University Physics? A Performance Comparison of AI Chatbots
    Vı́ctor Robledo-Rella, Andrés Gonzalez-Nucamendi, Luis Neri, Rosa M.G. Garcı́a-Castelán, Julieta Noguez, and Jorge Valverde-Rebaza
    In 2025 IEEE Global Engineering Education Conference (EDUCON), 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Generative artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming the learning and teaching landscape, and large language models, such as ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot, are demonstrating their outstanding ability to hold conversations and generate relevant content. However, given their probabilistic nature, it is currently not possible to trust completely in their academic performance. A detailed comparison of the accuracy of two AI chatbots, ChatGPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro is presented by measuring their ability to correctly answer multiple-choice questions of standard undergraduate physics questionnaires. A total sample of 790 questions was analyzed, covering 13 mechanics’ themes, from units and measurements to gravitation. Each chatbot was given the same prompt and was asked to solve each question. Their answers were compared to the correct answers, and an accuracy indicator was derived for each chatbot. In partial agreement with similar studies, it was found that ChatGPT achieved an overall accuracy of only 58 (±7) points (on a 0–100 scale), while Gemini achieved 79 (±6), which represents a 36% improvement. An analysis of variances proved this difference to be significant. The stability of ChatGPT was also tested through different runs, yielding basically the same performance. Its average accuracy may vary up to 11 points for a given theme. On the other hand, ChatGPT running with the Wolfram Alpha plugin did not achieve a systematic improvement, and its average accuracy may vary up to 17 points for a given theme. A PhysicsGPT by pulsr.co.uk from the OpenAI gallery was also tested, and it only achieved an overall accuracy of 57. When using these AI chatbots to solve multiple-choice questions in undergraduate physics topics requiring mathematical procedures, teachers and students should take these results as a warning about their current lack of precision and potential misleadingness.

  3. Front. Educ.
    A comparative study of a new challenge-based learning model for engineering majors
    V. Robledo-Rella, L. Neri, R.M.G. Garcı́a-Castelán, A. Gonzalez-Nucamendi, J. Valverde-Rebaza, and J. Noguez
    Frontiers in Education, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Current society needs professionals able to identify and collaboratively solve the main challenges of the 21st century. Students must be trained to face real challenges in their future workplaces and to acquire the necessary competencies to succeed in the current-day global world. As an answer to such needs, the Tecnologico de Monterrey launched in 2019 its competency-based Tec21 educational model, emphasizing the development of disciplinary and transversal competencies using a challenge-based learning (CBL) model. The present contribution is aimed at measuring the effectiveness of this model compared to the more traditional previous learning (PL) model. We present a quasi-experimental study comparing the academic performance of 1705 freshman engineering students, 43% enrolled in the PL model and 57% enrolled in the CBL model. Although the CBL model was applied to all the institution’s majors, the paper is focused on the freshman physics courses of the engineering majors. The study spans seven semesters, from Spring 2018 to Spring 2021. It was found that the overall student performance, measured by the average final course grades, improved by 9.4% in the CBL model compared to the PL model. On the other hand, the challenge average grades of the CBL model were like the project average grades of the PL model. Additionally, two-year opinion surveys about the CBL model were administered to 570 students. It was found that 71% of the students expressed a favorable perception of the CBL model in terms of their competencies development and their ability to solve problems. It is suggested that the explicit integration of physics, math, and computing concepts through the solution of real-life challenges in the CBL model bears a stronger student engagement and thus yields better general learning outcomes than the PL model.

    A Factor Analysis of Student Motivations of the Tecnologico de Monterrey Leaders of Tomorrow Scholarship
    A. Gonzalez-Nucamendi, L.J. Neri-Vitela, V. Robledo-Rella, R.M.G. Garcı́a-Castelán, J. Noguez, and J.C. Valverde-Rebaza
    In ICERI2024 Proceedings, Nov 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Since 2013, the ’Leaders of Tomorrow’ Scholarship Program at Tecnologico de Monterrey, Mexico, has fully supported academically outstanding university students from economically disadvantaged backgrounds, who have shown a strong commitment to leadership in social impact initiatives. Our previous research explored characteristics contributing to student academic success, including participation in group activities, community projects, technological or research developments, and sociodemographic factors. However, beyond individual achievement, it is crucial to evaluate the broader societal impact of their academic training. Specifically, it is important to consider how students utilize their education to contribute positively to their communities and society at large. This study investigates the motivations of 1,200 students who enrolled in the scholarship program between 2017 and 2022. The original database, provided by the Institute for the Future of Education at Tecnologico de Monterrey, consisted of 22,718 records. The main objective of this study is to identify the motivational profiles of these academically successful students and understand the factors that drive them to continue their education at the university level. To achieve this, Spranger’s theory is used as a foundation, suggesting that everyone possesses a unique set of motivations. Six fundamental motivational values—aesthetic, economic, individualistic, political, altruistic, and theoretical—were analyzed to uncover underlying factors using the statistical tool called Factor Analysis. During the admission process for the scholarship program, students were assessed to estimate their six motivational indices, each on a scale from 1 to 100. The results of the Factor Analysis, including three factors and the factor loads for their most representative variables, are presented below: Factor 1. Socioeconomic Dimension: This dimension is characterized by high altruism (0.851) and low concern for economic (-0.717) and political (-0.582) benefits. It reflects a balance between a strong desire to help others and a lesser focus on obtaining economic or political advantages. Factor 2. Theoretical-Intellectual Dimension: Characterized by a strong orientation towards theoretical knowledge (0.984), highlighting an inclination for abstract thinking. Factor 3. Aesthetic-Individual Dimension: Focuses on beauty and self-expression, with high aesthetic (0.839) and moderate individualistic (0.394), indicating a significant appreciation for creativity and personal autonomy. These three factors account for 69.2% of the total variance in the motivational indices measured, providing a comprehensive view of the motivational profiles of these students. By considering the deep motivations and individual needs of students, we can tailor educational support to achieve more equitable access to higher education. Understanding these motivations allows us to design specific interventions that address needs, attracting talented students to high-quality education. This approach aids students in achieving their academic goals and contributes to creating a fairer and more equitable society. Additionally, studying these motivations provides valuable insights for strengthening scholarship programs both in Latin America and worldwide, allowing better adaptation to support students’ ambitions.
